## **Brass Inserts for 3D Printing**

[This video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KC1LLU54DKU) by *Geek Detour* & *My Tech Fun* is an excellent introduction to using brass inserts with 3D printed components. While not strictly required to get a clean or solid assembly, it does certainly make the job much easier. 

Brass inserts are generally named in the following way: Ma^b^c where a represents the width of the screw the insert is rated for, i.e. a M4 insert is made for a 4mm screw, b represents the height of the insert in mm, and c represents the diameter of the insert in millimeters. So an M6^8^8 insert is rated for 6mm screws, is 8mm tall, and is 8mm in diameter. 

Generally, when sizing out the holes for the inserts on a 3D model, you can take away 0.5mm from the diameter and add 0.5mm to the height. So, to continue the previous example, the guide hole for the insert would be 7.5mm wide and 8.5mm tall. The reason we shrink the hole slightly is to give the inserts more material to fully grip onto, ensuring a much snugger fit. 

It is also recommended, although not necessary, to add an additional circle 1mm wider than the diameter of the insert, so 9mm in this case, with a depth of 0.1mm. This gives you a slight divot to help align the insert and hold in place while setting it. 

## **Breadboards and other Components**

Unless you all decide to go a very interesting and unexpected direction with this project this week, we will likely not be working with any breadboards or other board type components that need anything directly soldered onto them. However, the basic principles of soldering both wires and ports apply. Generally, just be careful while trying to keep your solders smooth and cleaning up any excess materials.
